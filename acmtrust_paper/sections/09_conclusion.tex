\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A generated simulator that reproduces recorded history has not shown that it handles the change a what-if question asks about. Testing the requested change before releasing the answer closes much of that gap. On programs written by Claude models, the history check let \rHistWrongClaude{} wrong answers through and Vouch let \rGateWrongClaude, nearly all of the difference coming from one platform and most of it from changed routing probabilities. Vouch found these errors through failed tests rather than abstention, and its record of past failures saved program runs without preventing any error. It needed no extra model calls, and at equal coverage accepted fewer wrong answers than LLM judges built on Claude Haiku.

The benefit is conditional. Where the history check already finds the errors, or programs make none or are mostly broken, Vouch adds little, and it always costs some correct answers. After a policy change it answered nothing, while agreement with a reading answer kept nearly half of the answers. Our results suggest judging each answer by the change it depends on, reporting why it was declined, and comparing checks at equal coverage and per platform. Open questions are how to recover useful answers once recorded history no longer applies, and whether the gain holds for faults outside the kinds of change our tests target.
